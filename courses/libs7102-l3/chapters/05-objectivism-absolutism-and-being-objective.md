Objectivist, objective and absolutist mean three different things, and the web's definitions are not the course's.

## Believing in universal values says nothing about which values are right

Slide 23: Paul believes values A + B are universal, Sarah B + C, Michael *believes there are no universal values*. Who is right? *We don't know. We just know that Paul and Sarah are objectivists. Michael is a relativist (no universal values) or moral nihilist (no values).*

## Being a moral objectivist is not being objective

Slide 24: *To be a moral objectivist is to subscribe to a philosophical position – moral objectivism = to say that moral relativism is wrong.* *To say that someone is "objective" is instead to describe their psychology – they are impartial, unbiased.* *Moral objectivists can be biased*: the UAW and Tesla *both seem to be objectivists*, but unbiased? *Probably not.* *Both Hitler and Gandhi were moral objectivists.*

## Empirical claims about norms are not normative claims

Slide 26 lists three distinctions: descriptive vs normative relativism, relativism vs objectivism, objectivism vs absolutism. Slide 27: *"Europeans consider that earning money from phishing and hacking is morally OK"* is an empirical claim about norms; *"I personally consider hacking wrong, as I'm Canadian, but if you don't live in Canada do whatever your culture says is right"* is a normative (relativist) one. *Someone being an objectivist says nothing of their impartiality*: the KKK members' son who says *"everyone around the world should be as racist as possible"* is an objectivist, and not impartial.

## Pojman: objectivism and absolutism are different claims

Slide 28 rejects the *myth* that *objectivists are those who follow hard rules*: *objectivists can be absolutist (Kantians) and non-absolutist (e.g. utilitarians, virtue ethics)*. Learn Pojman's definitions word for word:

:::exam Pojman's definitions (slide 28)
*Moral objectivism: There is a fact of the matter as to whether any given action is morally permissible or impermissible: a fact of the matter that does not depend solely on social custom or individual acceptance.*

*Moral absolutism: There is at least one principle that ought never to be violated.*
:::

Absolutist objectivism adds that right and wrong *can be captured in principles*. Slide 29: *"Canadians shouldn't lie!"* is absolutist and relativist at once, so *stating "It Depends"* does not commit anyone to *being a relativist*.

## Loyalty and nationality can collapse a claim into relativism

Slides 30 and 31 sort four people:

| Who | Says | Slide's verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Michael, a white supremacist | all programmers should be white | objectivist, not psychologically objective |
| Helen, whose father's unpaid work is hidden by an NDA | this *should happen to no one anywhere* | objectivist, perhaps not objective, *since it's her father* |
| Riley | *"If you work for Amazon, you need to fully support whatever it does"* | *likely relativist and probably psychologically biased* |
| Edmonton workers unionizing (2022) | it *matches Canadian values* | *probably relativist*: may others exploit outside Canada? |

Loyalty is not intrinsically wrong, but *blind loyalty turns out to be a form of relativism*. Slide 32 judges three claims, all *Wrong*: stealing is fine from a big company but not a start-up (*objectivist claim that responds to circumstances*); sharing a client's data if your company doesn't care (*relativist*); planting a virus out of loyalty (*objectivist on the surface, but blind loyalty at all costs ends up collapsing into relativism*).

## "It depends" on circumstances is not relativism

Slide 33: *Don't confuse a resistance to absolutism (context/circumstances matter) with relativism (cultural context is all that matters).* The engineer who uses different bridge designs *is rejecting absolutism, but they are not a relativist about physics.* Lecture 4 slide 6's *Common Mistake*: relativism depends on *the culture/opinion where the judgment is made*, non-absolutism on *the relevant circumstances of the action*.
