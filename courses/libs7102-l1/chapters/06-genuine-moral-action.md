title: "Self-interest is not selfishness, so genuine moral action exists"
minutes: 20

If everyone is always selfish, there is no genuine moral action. Slides 31 to 41 answer with Hume and Williams.

## Glaucon says we are moral only because we must

Slide 31: *Many have argued that genuine moral action is actually an illusion. They claim we act in ways that look moral because we're trying to get something from it (instrumental value)*: *1. money; 2. reciprocal favours or avoiding punishment; 3. the admiration of others; 4. pleasure of believing in the illusion we're pure kind people.* In Plato's *Republic*, Glaucon tells the story of Gyges' ring: someone with a ring that makes them invisible at will *would never act according to moral rules*: we are just *"of necessity"*.

Slide 33 names the two distinctions needed: *selfishness vs self-interest*, and *psychological egoism and why we should reject it*.

## Psychological egoism describes; normative egoism prescribes

Slide 34 quotes Sober and Wilson: *"[Psychological] Egoism maintains that when we care about what happens to others, we do so only as a means to increasing our own welfare [...] In all our social interactions, we are driven by a single question – 'What's in it for me?'"* Sober & Wilson, *Unto Others* (1998: 2).

- *Psychological egoism: the theory/view that everyone is self-interested at all times (an empirical claim).*
- *Normative egoism: The theory/view that everyone ought to always act out of self-interest (a value/normative claim).* The course notes name Ayn Rand as a famous normative egoist.

Slide 35: Hobbes held psychological egoism, *"[O]f the voluntary acts of every man, the [ultimate] object is some good to himself"*, and reduced pity to self-interest: *"Grief for the calamity of another is pity; and ariseth from the imagination that the like calamity may befall himself"*.

## Hume: benevolence is natural, so moral or selfish is a false dichotomy

Slide 36: we sometimes deceive ourselves that we are nice, *but always? No!* Of *a man, that grieves for a valuable friend, who needed his patronage and protection*: *how can we suppose that his passionate tenderness arises from some metaphysical regards to a self-interest, which has no foundation or reality?* *What interest can a fond mother have in view, who loses her health by assiduous attendance on her sick child, and afterwards languishes and dies of grief, when freed, by his death, from the slavery of that attendance?*

Slide 37: *Benevolence is a natural tendency of human beings*. Everyone has some, *even the most vicious among us*, unequally distributed and *mostly directed to those close to us*. *We often thus fail to be moral not by being selfish, but by directing our benevolence incorrectly.* *So the dichotomy moral/selfish is a false dichotomy*: *everyone is selfish sometimes, but everyone is also benevolent at least sometimes.* Slide 41's example: nepotism, benevolence where we should have been fair.

Slide 38: *To desire is not the same as to be selfish. To be selfish is rather to have selfish desires.* *What matters is what they desire! – things for themselves only or also things for others?*

## Selfishness is excessive concern with self-interest

Slide 39: *You are not selfish if you tie your shoes to avoid tripping, or sleep because you're tired.* *But what if I, not being particularly thirsty, have two glasses of water, when someone next to me is dying of thirst?* *Selfishness is then excessive concern with one's self-interest.* Lecture 2 slide 9: *eating is caring for one's self-interest*; *eating someone else's lunch is also self-interested*.

On enjoying a moral act, Aristotle (lecture 2 slide 10): *The truly virtuous enjoys moral action! That's evidence of greater virtue, not less*, unless enjoyment is the primary or single reason. Slide 11: "kindness is just programmed in us by evolution" would also make love an illusion and sunsets without beauty: *careful not to conflate the origin of motivations (the history of how moral emotions came to be)* with *the genuineness of motivations (the psychology)*.

Slide 40 concludes: when we fail to be moral it is often *out of (a flawed sense of) loyalty/love* (*We mean well*); *It's inaccurate to say that everyone is selfish*; *Genuine moral action does exist.*
