title: Utilitarianism maximizes total utility, not happiness for the majority or benefits over costs

Slides 5 to 10 are the instructor's revisions: common essay mistakes about act utilitarianism, each one corrected.

## Slide 5 lists what keeps an essay's grade low

Slide 5 gives the *typical issues that come up in essays*:

- *No consideration of objections* (lecture 2).
- *Summarizing utilitarianism down to one sentence or two* (e.g. *"it's about aiming for the general good"*).
- *Quotes on utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill) not accompanied by an explanation in your own words.*
- *Complex calculations of utility (X causes pleasure, Y causes pain, Z would cause even more pleasure, ...) do little to show knowledge of the theory.*
- *Critical discussion of problematic features (such as the ones we cover today) does a lot more to show knowledge.*
- *Engage with complex issues successfully.*
- *The approach of avoiding mistakes by only dealing with the basics guarantees a low grade.*

## Utilitarianism maximizes total aggregated utility, not utility for the majority

Slide 6 says a student's *"We should maximize utility for the majority"* is wrong: *Is this correct? No.* The mistake comes from Bentham's *A Fragment on Government* (1776), with its *"fundamental axiom, it is the greatest happiness of the greatest number that is the measure of right and wrong"*: the misread quote of [lecture 4](#/libs7102-l4/equal-consideration-of-interests). Two clarifications:

1. *Utilitarianism in fact does not say we must serve the majority. It just so happens that in many cases (but not all) utility adds up when high numbers of people are found (e.g. kill 1 to save 19).*
2. *Utilitarianism doesn't recommend uniform and wider distribution of utility so we can serve a "greater number".*

The rule: *What matters is only total aggregated utility. Choose that option no matter how many are helped, and how distribution looks.*

## Utilitarianism is not a cost-benefit analysis

Slide 7 calls a student's *"it's moral because the benefits outweigh the costs"* wrong: *This is an inaccurate summary of the actual procedure*, because *you may have several options where the benefits outweigh the costs*, and *you may have no options where this happens*.

*Utilitarianism is not a cost-benefit analysis. The latter has a point of view. What costs and benefits for X?* The slide's example is a car maker's *Cost Benefit Analysis*, counted from its own side: 180 burn deaths, 180 serious burns and 2100 Pintos burned, at $200 000 per death, $67 000 per serious injury and $700 per car, total $49.5 million, against $11 per vehicle (total $137 million) for the safety alteration or $5.08 per vehicle (about $64 million) for a rubber bladder.

Utilitarianism counts everyone equally, from no one's side. And *utilitarianism is also not about causing more good than bad*.

## The closest short definition is a three-step algorithm

Slide 8 answers the student's *What's the right one??*: *We never ask for super short single-sentence definitions. Utilitarianism cannot be summarized in a single sentence. Use the whole essay to show your knowledge.* *The closest and still incomplete "short" definition will perhaps be to describe the "algorithm" and implement it:*

1. *identify all affected by available options for action*
2. *for each available action, aggregate all relevant utility (since the theory subscribes to normative hedonism, this is the sum of pleasure and pain, using ECI) considering the actual outcome* and
3. *choose the option that maximizes utility*

## It is called act utilitarianism because it judges actions, not rules

Slide 9 rejects *"Respecting consent maximizes utility (as opposed to never respecting it), therefore we must always respect consent"*: *Is this what act utilitarianism says? No.* *Respecting (all) forms of privacy is not an action (i.e. it's a class of actions). Act utilitarianism is not absolutist and evaluates each action on its own merits.* The student's sentence *would be rule utilitarianism – choose the rule (not action) that maximizes utility*. Rule utilitarianism is *often accused of collapsing into act utilitarianism, as an even better rule for maximizing utility would be "respect consent unless in a specific case you can maximize utility through a breach of consent, such as using dark patterns"*.

## An action is not right merely because it causes happiness

Slide 10 says a student's *"The action is right because it causes happiness"* is wrong. The source is Mill: *"Actions are right in proportion as they tend to promote happiness"*, the *Proportionality doctrine of the Greatest Happiness Principle*. *You may infer from the quote that a) right actions always cause happiness, b) there are many right actions and c) an action is right if it increases happiness. All incorrect.* The clarification:

- *A) The action recommended by utilitarianism may cause pain.*
- *B) Increasing utility is not enough if it's not maximized. It's the "greatest" happiness principle.*
