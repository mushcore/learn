Divine Orb prices from two leagues, one at nearly twice the other's level, cannot be compared by their standard deviations alone. $s$ and $\sigma$ tell you how spread out $X$ is, in the **same units as $X$**: an **absolute** measure of variation. To compare the *relative* spread of two variables with different units, or wildly different scales, you need the **coefficient of variation**.

## The formula

$$CV = \frac{s}{\bar{X}} \times 100\%$$

That's the sample version. For a population, swap in $\sigma$ and $\mu$: $CV = \dfrac{\sigma}{\mu} \times 100\%$.

A 10-chaos wobble on a 100-chaos item is $10/100 = 10\%$ of its price; the same 10-chaos wobble on a 300-chaos item is $10/300 = 3.3\%$. Same $s$, a third of the relative spread.

CV expresses the standard deviation **as a percentage of the mean**. Because $s$ and $\bar{X}$ share the units of $X$, the units cancel and $CV$ is **unitless**, which is what lets you compare height in cm against age in years, or two funds measured in different currencies.

:::tip Reading CV
Smaller CV = more consistent. Compare CVs, not $s$, whenever the means differ.
:::

## Worked example from the lecture: height vs age

Height: $s = 5.2$ cm, $\bar{X} = 165$ cm.

$CV_{height} = \frac{5.2}{165} \times 100\% = 3.15\%$

Age: $s = 3.5$ years, $\bar{X} = 20.2$ years.

$CV_{age} = \frac{3.5}{20.2} \times 100\% = 17.3\%$

$3.5 < 5.2$ in raw units, so age looks less variable at first glance; divided by its much smaller mean, age has the **much higher relative variation**. When the means differ, comparing $s$ values directly is wrong: compute $CV$ for each. A smaller $s$ can still mean a **larger** $CV$.

## Lab 1 pencil problem 4: Hank vs Pete

Two students' quiz scores: Hank $62 \pm 4.2$, Pete $59 \pm 4.1$ (mean $\pm$ sd).

$CV_{Hank} = \frac{4.2}{62}\times 100\% = 6.774\%$, $CV_{Pete} = \frac{4.1}{59}\times 100\% = 6.949\%$

Pete's raw $s = 4.1$ is smaller than Hank's $s = 4.2$, but Pete's $CV$ is **larger**. Relative to his own mean, Pete's scores bounce around more — so **Hank is the more consistent** of the two.

## Lab 1 pencil problem 7a: Site A vs Site B

Site A: mean $4.39$, $s = 2.5836$. Site B: mean $4.0783$, $s = 3.1421$.

$CV_A = \frac{2.5836}{4.39}\times 100\% = 58.85\%$, $CV_B = \frac{3.1421}{4.0783}\times 100\% = 77.04\%$

Site A has both the higher mean and the lower $CV$, so Site A is **higher and more consistent** than Site B.
