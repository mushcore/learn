If morality were a list of rules to memorize, nothing would develop. Kohlberg, after Piaget, found that moral reasoning grows in stages, and many adults stop halfway.

## Piaget: young children judge by consequences, older children by intentions

Slide 13, *Piaget's 2 stages of moral development inspired Kohlberg*:

- *Children younger than 10/11 – rules are fixed and absolute, handed down by parents or God. Judgment based on consequences of actions.*
- *Children older than 10/11 – rules can be changed if everyone agrees, and are tools for getting along. Judgment based on intentions behind actions.*

The 15 cups (Piaget, 1932): told of *one boy who broke 15 cups trying to help his mother and another boy who broke only one cup trying to steal cookies, the young child thinks that the first boy did worse*. The young child counts cups; the older child looks at intentions.

## Moral maturity is a skill, not a memorized list

Slide 14: stages suggest *that the ability of being moral is not the ability of memorizing certain claims (e.g. "lying is wrong")*; *moral knowledge could be know-how/a skill rather than knowing-that*. *Capuchin monkeys, for instance, seem to be able to acquire a sense of fairness – a know-how* (the photo shows two capuchins in neighbouring cages).

## Each stage has its own reason to do right and its own way to go wrong

Slides 17 to 23 take one stage each, most asking *Example of how it could go wrong?*

| Stage (slide) | The slide's words | How it could go wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Obedience and punishment (17) | *Similar to Piaget's stage 1*; norms *handed down from figures of power*; stealing is bad *"because you'll get punished"*; *"Might makes right"* | copying licensed code when no audit would catch it |
| 2 Instrumental exchange (18) | *relativists, thinking that it's right whatever suits one's interests - normative egoism*, with *a sense of fair exchange* | fixing a colleague's bug only if they return the favour |
| 3 Good boy/nice girl (19) | *individuals react to rewards. They want to be praised*; *live up to expectations of others, typically family members and close friends*; *be worthy of the praise we get* | approving a bad merge to look like a team player |
| 4 Law and order, the good citizen (20) | *Concern with society as a whole*; adds *laws are useful for social order*; *"Crime requires punishment. Must pay debt to society."*; *Authority figures are seldom questioned* | practice question K2 below |
| 4.5 The cynic (21) | conventions *relative and arbitrary*, *but no alternative is known*; *"do your own thing"*; hippies; *"all politicians are liars"*, *ironically both rebellious and conventional*; *Wise enough to question it all, not wise enough to find answers* | "every ethics code is PR, so anything goes" |
| 5 Social contract and individual rights (22) | *Laws are not good simply because they are laws*; *changing laws through democratic agreements*; *human rights, and deterrence* | *Why do we need another level beyond this?* (next lesson) |
| 6 Universal ethical principles (23) | *The "prophet" stage*; *Lists of moral rules may be abandoned in favour of deeper wisdom* | next lesson |

Slide 20 marks the step to stage 4: conventional morality *adds justification - laws are useful for social order* to the rule. And: *Many adults never reach the third level (stages 5 and 6)*. The course notes agree that development *tends to stall at the conventional level of stages three and four*; Kohlberg's post-conventional examples are *Mohandas Gandhi or Martin Luther King, Jr.*
